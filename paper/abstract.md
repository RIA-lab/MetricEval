# Abstract {.unnumbered}

As designs outnumber what a laboratory can test, in-silico metrics decide which enzymes are built, yet it is not known whether they respond to catalysis or to something simpler, such as proximity to the active site. We tested 33 metrics, alongside trivial references, for what a metric that tracks catalysis should do: separate active from inactive enzymes, tell a cognate substrate from a wrong one, rank enzymes by activity, and register damage to the catalytic residues more than the same damage elsewhere. No metric separated natural dead enzymes from their active forms beyond chance, although the pLDDT of the whole prediction did (AUROC 0.79), and on 192 designs the tyrosine fraction came close to the best metric (0.75 against 0.80). ipTM separated the cognate ligand from a wrong one (0.70), but the RMSD of the catalytic atoms barely did. The distance from the mutated residue to the catalytic residues tracked the loss of activity of enzyme variants as well as the best metric (ρ = 0.46 against 0.48), and the metric most responsive to catalytic damage fell from 0.75 to 0.59 (0.5 = no preference) when the control lay at the same distance from the ligand.
