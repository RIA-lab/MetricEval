# Abstract {.unnumbered}

As designs outnumber what a laboratory can test, in-silico metrics decide which enzymes are built. Pipelines filter on many different metrics, from Rosetta energies and the geometry of the catalytic residues to the confidence of structure-prediction models. Yet no systematic study has asked whether these metrics respond to catalysis or to something simpler, such as proximity to the active site. We therefore designed four tests and applied them to 33 metrics. *Activity discrimination* asks whether a metric separates enzymes that work from enzymes that do not; *substrate discrimination*, whether it knows which substrate an enzyme is built for; *activity ranking*, whether it orders enzymes by their activity; and *detection ability*, whether it notices damage to the catalytic residues more than the same damage elsewhere. Three tests observe natural enzymes and designs as they are, whereas detection ability damages natural enzymes on purpose, so that catalysis can be told apart from proximity to the active site. Every test ranks the metrics beside trivial references, such as the distance to the catalytic residues. In activity discrimination, pLDDT was best on natural enzymes, with an AUROC of 0.79, and the intra-residue repulsion of the catalytic residues on designs, with 0.80. ipTM was best in substrate discrimination, with 0.70, the range of the catalytic pKa in activity ranking, with a Spearman correlation of 0.48, and the per-chain pTM minimum in detection ability, with a probability of 0.75 of changing more at the catalytic residues than at the control. <!-- src: summary of the Introduction (paragraphs 1 to 6) and the Discussion; 33 metrics (Methods 2.1); best item per test, numbers from tables/public/main: T5 (pLDDT 0.79 on the 21 natural pairs), T6 (intra-residue repulsion 0.80 on the 192 designs), T7 (ipTM mean AUROC 0.70), T8 (catalytic pKa range, Spearman rho 0.48), T10 (per-chain pTM minimum, mean R 0.75) -->
