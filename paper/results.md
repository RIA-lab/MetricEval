# 3 Results

The four subsections follow the four experiments of the Methods (2.1 → 3.1, 2.2 → 3.2, 2.3 → 3.3, 2.4 → 3.4). Counts are of distinct metrics in the main set of 34 (29 structure-space, 5 prediction-based, Box 2 of the Methods), written as *k of N*. Intervals are 95% bootstrap intervals that resample the clustering unit named in each subsection, unless a different level is stated. Every number cites its table or result file in a hidden `src` comment; the full per-metric tables are in the Supplement. Perturbation response is not activity prediction, and nothing below should be read as a claim that a metric predicts activity.

## 3.1 Structure-space metrics respond to catalytic damage, and separate it from matched damage only when the lesion is large

**The panel sees the damage at every step.** At the isosteric step, 23 of 29 structure-space metrics respond to the substitution at a catalytic residue (the interval of the median change excludes zero); at the Gly step, which is also the detection floor, 19 of the 26 metrics that are defined respond (Table 3). Detection is not the problem. <!-- src: tables/main/T3_part1A_structure_space.csv (isosteric: 23 of 29; gly: 19 of 26); tables/supp/S05_N0_by_input_class.csv (N0 cat→Gly identical to the C-Gly rung) -->

**No metric is specific at the isosteric step.** None of the 29 metrics responds more at a catalytic residue than at the matched control: 0 of 15 Rosetta energy terms, 0 of 8 geometry metrics, 0 of 6 pKa metrics (Table 3). The panel as a whole sits at a specificity ratio of 1.07 (geometric mean over the 27 metrics with a defined ratio; 90% interval [0.96, 1.18], nested bootstrap over 74 enzyme sub-subclasses), inside the ±25% equivalence band. Without packing matching and arm-count gating the value is 1.02 [0.91, 1.12]. The earlier whole-panel statistic (0.995 [0.937, 1.134], 102 metrics) included about 34 composition metrics whose ratio is 1 by construction, so we report the main-set value. <!-- src: tables/main/T3_part1A_structure_space.csv (P2K_C isosteric 0 of 29; panel statistic row); tables/spec/derived/panel_equivalence_main.json; tables/supp/S11_equivalence_legacy_vs_main.csv -->

**Specificity appears for 5 to 8 metrics once the lesion exceeds 36 Å³.** At the non-isosteric step (36.4 Å³) 5 of 29 metrics are specific, at Ala (53.2 Å³) 8 of 29 and at Gly (80.5 Å³) 8 of 26 (Table 3). The largest ratios belong to burial descriptors: relative solvent-accessible area (mean over the changed residues) has SR 1.86 [1.50, 2.34] at the non-isosteric step, 2.40 [1.93, 3.03] at Ala and 2.22 [1.71, 2.67] at Gly, and the fraction buried 1.91 [1.40, 2.72] at Ala and 2.00 [1.67, 2.33] at Gly. A few Rosetta terms follow with much smaller ratios: lk-ball solvation 1.85 [1.27, 2.58] at Ala and 1.60 [1.21, 2.30] at Gly, and attraction, repulsion, rotamer and solvation terms between 1.14 and 1.32 with intervals that begin just above 1. None of the six pKa metrics is specific at any step, and they do not respond at all at Ala or Gly (0 of 6). Removing 1, 2 or 4 second-shell residues makes no metric specific (0 of 29 at each dose; 0, 0 and 2 metrics respond). <!-- src: tables/supp/S04a_part1A_eligible_metrics.csv (SR median and 95% interval per cell); tables/main/T3_part1A_structure_space.csv (P2KE_E rows) -->

**What the control does and does not rule out.** The control arm perturbed fewer residues than the catalytic arm in 24.7% of 563 (enzyme, step) pairs; those comparisons are handled by the count gate, and the match tiers are in Table S6. ## 3.2 Prediction-based metrics know that the ligand changed, and their response to a catalytic substitution is not specific once distance is controlled

**Substrate swap.** In the 59 natural enzymes, the Chai-1 interface terms separate the cognate ligand from a wrong ligand moderately and the active-site accuracy does not (Table 4). ipTM has a direction-free AUROC of 0.70 [0.61, 0.79] for a wrong ligand of the same enzyme class, 0.74 [0.66, 0.83] for one of a different class and 0.67 [0.57, 0.77] for a decoy; the aggregate score is nearly identical, and the per-chain pTM minimum is weaker (0.58 [0.51, 0.67], 0.62 [0.51, 0.72], and 0.50 against the decoy). The AME RMSD is at chance for every wrong ligand (0.52 to 0.53, upper bounds at most 0.57). A same-class ligand is almost as detectable as a different-class one, so the metric registers that the ligand changed and little about how different the chemistry is. The wrong ligands are chosen by us, so these AUROCs describe sensitivity to the ligand supplied, not a ranking of designs. <!-- src: results/P5/REPORT.md (substrate-swap tables, natural); tables/main/T4_part1B_prediction_based.csv (P5_S_NAT rows) -->

**Ligand size accounts for part of it.** The probability that ipTM is higher for the cognate than for the wrong ligand is 0.75 [0.65, 0.84] over all 171 cells in 58 systems, 1.00 when the wrong ligand has six or more heavy atoms fewer, 0.76 [0.62, 0.90] when the sizes are matched, and 0.48 [0.32, 0.66] when the wrong ligand is larger. Across cells, the size difference correlates with the change in ipTM at Spearman 0.67 [0.53, 0.78]. At matched size and charge (33 cells, 26 systems) the win fraction is 0.85 [0.71, 0.97], so some discrimination survives the control. In the 30 de novo designs ipTM discriminates strongly (AUROC 0.87 [0.79, 0.94] to 0.91 [0.84, 0.97]); the paired win fraction is 0.92 [0.84, 0.99] over all 87 cells and holds in every size stratum, but charge cannot be controlled there (6 of 87 cells have equal charge). The active-site metric for designs is the self-consistency flavour and is reported in the Supplement, never pooled with the crystal flavour. <!-- src: results/P5_1B/REPORT.md summary items 6 and 7; tables/supp/S14b_substrate_swap_size_strata.csv -->

**Much of the catalytic ratio of the interface terms is distance.** In the chemistry ladder, re-predicted for 74 systems, the original control sat a median 13.4 Å from the ligand while the catalytic residue it was paired with sat 3.1 Å away (266 natural pairs; only 24 pairs within 2 Å of each other). Measured against that control, the interface terms look specific: on the isosteric rung the mean-based SR is 1.96 [1.44, 2.71] for ipTM, 1.96 [1.45, 2.69] for the aggregate score and 1.57 [1.19, 3.21] for the per-chain pTM minimum. Adjusting for the distance to the ligand and burial removes 46 to 77% of the effect on the log scale (Table 4): ipTM falls to 1.18 [0.85, 1.60], the aggregate score to 1.22 [0.90, 1.61], the per-chain pTM minimum to 1.43 [0.99, 1.96] and the ligand clearance to 1.18 [0.95, 1.46]. All four intervals include 1. <!-- src: results/P5_1B/REPORT.md summary items 1 and 2; results/P5_1B/ladder_sr_adjusted.csv; tables/main/T4_part1B_prediction_based.csv -->

**No interface term is specific in the distance-matched subset.** Under the frozen rule none of the four interface terms is specific: the lower 90% bound of the mean-based SR is 0.27 for ipTM, the aggregate score and the per-chain pTM minimum, and 0.84 for the ligand clearance, against the 1.2 the rule requires (68 pairs in 21 systems for each interface term; 69 pairs in 22 systems for the AME RMSD). The point estimate for ipTM in this subset is 0.47 [0.24, 1.04]: in distance-matched pairs the control moved ipTM more, on average, than the catalytic substitution did. The interval includes 1, so this is not a finding that catalytic residues matter less. The AME RMSD, which measures where the remaining catalytic residues end up, has an adjusted ratio of 1.90 [1.40, 2.53] with the original controls and 1.68 [1.25, 2.23] with the new ones, and 0.98 [0.68, 1.21] in the matched subset. It receives no verdict, because the control is matched on distance to the ligand and not on distance to the other catalytic residues. <!-- src: results/P5_1B/REPORT.md summary items 3, 4 and 5; results/P5_1B/ladder_sr_matched.csv -->

**Limits specific to this experiment.** The matched subset is a subpopulation: a same-type, buried, non-adjacent residue within 2 Å of the catalytic residue's ligand distance exists for 75 of 312 pairs, and 213 pairs have no candidate anywhere in their protein. Each ladder prediction uses one seed. A second seed on 30 natural pairs shows that the control-arm change in ipTM is at the noise floor (σ of the change 0.0080 against a median change of 0.0030, r = 0.75 between seeds) while the catalytic-arm change is reproducible (0.0053 against 0.0052, r = 0.98), so the denominator of the SR is noise-limited and a single-seed SR on those 30 pairs moves by about a quarter to a third between seeds (ipTM 2.35 against 3.12). <!-- src: results/P5_1B/REPORT.md sections 4, 6 and 7; results/P5_1B/replicate_noise_floor.csv; results/P5_1B/replicate_sr_seed_comparison.csv -->

## 3.3 No structure-space metric separates real dead enzymes from active ones better than a reference row

**Structure-space metrics.** Across the 49 zymogen–mature pairs (49 proteins), none of the 29 main-set metrics reaches an AUROC of 0.8 and none exceeds the confound floor of 0.655 (Table 5). The median AUROC is 0.52 and the highest is 0.57 [0.51, 0.66], for the omega-angle term of the site's Rosetta energy. Over all 136 metrics scored on these pairs none reaches 0.8; the highest value is 0.61, for crystallographic resolution, a reference row that needs no catalytic information. The catalytic constellation is nearly the same in the two forms (median RMSD 0.207 Å after superposing the constellation, 0.484 Å in the backbone frame), so the pairs isolate catalytic competence from catalytic chemistry. <!-- src: tables/main/T5_part2A_real_dead_active.csv; results/P0/metric_separation.csv; results/P0/REPORT.md (median RMSD table) -->

**Prediction-based metrics.** On the 28 zymogen pairs that were re-predicted, the AME RMSD has an AUROC of 0.51 [0.50, 0.61]. The interface terms, defined only on the 21 pairs whose two forms share a ligand, give 0.52 to 0.54; those values were computed before the definedness gate and include apo pairs, so we do not interpret them. The two prediction reference rows are higher than every metric: pLDDT 0.77 [0.68, 0.86] and pTM 0.71 [0.61, 0.81]. The change in sequence length between zymogen and mature construct correlates with the change in pLDDT (−0.45), so these values are a length confound and not evidence about catalysis. <!-- src: tables/main/T5_part2A_real_dead_active.csv (P5T2_ZYM rows); results/P5/REPORT_T2.md -->

## 3.4 Against measured activity, the metrics carry a position signal that a distance baseline matches

**BglB structure-space metrics.** In 432 BglB variants at 175 positions (166 impaired, 254 wild-type-like), the size of the change in a catalytic-scoped metric separates impaired from wild-type-like variants for most of the 28 main-set metrics that are defined: 25 of 28 have an AUROC interval wholly above 0.5, with a median of 0.75 and a highest value of 0.77 [0.70, 0.83] for the range of the catalytic pKa (Table 6). The omega term is not defined on any of the 432 variants, because a side-chain substitution on a fixed backbone does not change it. The signal is a position signal. Across all 84 metrics analysed on BglB the median AUROC is 0.75 for catalytic-scoped metrics, 0.56 for whole-protein metrics and 0.50 for sequence-only metrics. The distance from the mutated residue to the catalytic glutamates, a quantity that needs no structure prediction, has an AUROC of 0.76 [0.69, 0.82], and none of the 28 main-set metrics beats it (the interval of the paired difference includes 0 for each). The other baselines are lower: burial 0.68 [0.61, 0.75], the dataset's own Rosetta score 0.61 [0.52, 0.70] (248 variants), BLOSUM62 0.59 [0.52, 0.65] and the volume change 0.55 [0.47, 0.63]. <!-- src: tables/main/T6_part2B_measured_effects.csv (D4_BGLB_M1 rows); results/D4_bglb/sensitivity_summary.csv (medians by input class) -->

**The result is stable and the signal shrinks within strata.** In 10 sensitivity analyses (a near and a distal stratum, restriction to variants with measured stability, dropping the five variants at catalytic positions, re-thresholding the labels at 1 and 3 standard deviations, restriction to variants whose metric responded, and leaving out each of three large institutions), no metric of the 84 analysed beats the distance baseline. Within the near stratum (variants within 10 Å of a catalytic residue; 144 variants at 39 positions) the distance baseline is 0.67 and the median catalytic-scoped AUROC 0.64; in the distal stratum (288 variants at 136 positions) the values are 0.65 and 0.62. <!-- src: results/D4_bglb/sensitivity_summary.csv (S1near, S1distal and the other sensitivity rows) -->

**BglB prediction-based metrics.** With the assay substrate present, the change in the Chai-1 interface terms tracks impairment at a similar level: ipTM has an AUROC of 0.70 [0.62, 0.77], the median of the five main-set metrics is 0.67, 4 of 5 have an interval above 0.5, and none beats the distance baseline (the lower end of the paired difference for ipTM is −0.12). The AME terms are weak (0.54 to 0.60 for the RMSD variants, against the covalent-intermediate crystal), and the pLDDT reference row is at chance (0.55 [0.48, 0.62]). <!-- src: tables/main/T6_part2B_measured_effects.csv (D4_BGLB_M3 rows); results/D4_bglb/m3/REPORT.md section 4 -->
