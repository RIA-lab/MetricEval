# 4 Discussion

As generative models produce more designs than any laboratory can test, the in-silico filter that chooses among them has become a decisive step of enzyme design. The designs that are built are the ones that pass it, and the same metrics increasingly decide which design methods are judged better. A filter that does not track catalysis therefore wastes experiments and can mislead the field about what works, yet the metrics behind these filters had not, to our knowledge, been tested against catalysis.

We tested 33 such metrics in four tests, each corresponding to a job that a filter is asked to do. Activity discrimination is the basic one, to separate designs that work from designs that do not. Substrate discrimination asks whether a metric knows what an enzyme is built for, which design conditioned on a substrate takes for granted. Activity ranking is the last step of a selection, choosing the best of the designs that pass. Detection ability asks for the property that makes a metric a measure of catalysis and not of geometry: it must register damage to the catalytic residues more than the same damage elsewhere in the protein. Three of the tests observe proteins as they exist and the fourth intervenes, which is what allows catalysis to be told apart from proximity. In every test a metric has to beat a trivial reference before it counts as having detected anything. A metric that tracks catalysis should do well in all four.

In activity discrimination, none of the 33 metrics separated natural dead enzymes from their active forms beyond chance (best AUROC 0.61, against a threshold of 0.68), whereas whole-prediction pLDDT did (0.79); on 192 designs the intra-residue repulsion of the catalytic residues led (0.80), with the tyrosine fraction close behind (0.75). In substrate discrimination, ipTM separated the cognate ligand from a wrong one (0.70), but the RMSD of the catalytic atoms barely did (0.53 to 0.56). In activity ranking, the range of the catalytic pKa correlated best with the measured impairment of BglB variants (ρ = 0.48), yet the distance from the mutated residue to the catalytic residues gives 0.46 and no metric beat it. In detection ability, the per-chain pTM minimum responded most to catalytic damage (R = 0.75) but fell to 0.59 when the control lay at the same distance from the ligand. Taken together, the metrics register damage close to the active site, or the supply of another ligand, and we could not show that they register whether the damage is catalytic. The designed sets are small and had already passed a filter, so this marks where the metrics fail to stand out, not that they carry no information.
