# 4 Discussion

As generative models produce more designs than any laboratory can test, the in-silico filter that chooses among them has become a decisive step of enzyme design. The designs that are built are the ones that pass it, and the same metrics increasingly decide which design methods are judged better. A filter that does not track catalysis therefore wastes experiments and can mislead the field about what works, yet the metrics behind these filters had not, to our knowledge, been tested against catalysis.

We tested 33 such metrics in four tests, each corresponding to a job that a filter is asked to do. Activity discrimination is the basic one, to separate designs that work from designs that do not. Substrate discrimination asks whether a metric knows what an enzyme is built for, which design conditioned on a substrate takes for granted. Activity ranking is the last step of a selection, choosing the best of the designs that pass. Detection ability asks for the property that makes a metric a measure of catalysis and not of geometry: it must register damage to the catalytic residues more than the same damage elsewhere in the protein. Three of the tests observe proteins as they exist and the fourth intervenes, which is what allows catalysis to be told apart from proximity. The tests set the metrics beside trivial references, so that each metric can be compared with something simple. A metric that tracks catalysis should do well in all four.

In activity discrimination, pLDDT of the whole prediction ranked first on natural dead and active enzymes (AUROC 0.79) and the best of the 33 metrics, the intra-residue repulsion of the catalytic residues, reached 0.61, below the chance threshold of 0.68; on 192 designs the intra-residue repulsion ranked first (0.80) and the tyrosine fraction reached 0.75. In substrate discrimination, ipTM ranked first (0.70) and the RMSD of the catalytic atoms reached 0.53 to 0.56. In activity ranking, the range of the catalytic pKa correlated best with the measured impairment of BglB variants (ρ = 0.48) and the distance from the mutated residue to the catalytic residues gave 0.46; no metric beat it. In detection ability, the per-chain pTM minimum had the highest mean R over the counted steps (0.75) and gave 0.59 when the control lay at the same distance from the ligand.
