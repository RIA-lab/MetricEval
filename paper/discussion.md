# 4 Discussion

**Principal finding.** The metrics that filter designed enzymes respond to where a change is made in a protein, and we could not show that they respond to whether the change is catalytic. Across four experiments with one panel of 33 metrics, the pattern is the same. On 21 zymogen pairs no main-set metric separates real dead enzymes from active ones beyond what chance gives the best of the list, and the quantities that do are whole-prediction confidence summaries; on 192 plated designs two repulsion terms of the catalytic residues exceed the best-of-36 threshold in the comparison of active against no active, as does a reference row, the tyrosine fraction, with intervals that are wide (3.1). When a structure predictor is given a wrong ligand, the interface terms separate it from the cognate one moderately and partly through ligand size, and the active-site accuracy barely does (3.2). On 432 measured variants of one enzyme the metrics rank the variants by their measured impairment with a rank correlation of about 0.45, which the distance from the mutated residue to the catalytic residues reproduces (0.46) without any structure at all (3.3). And under matched perturbation they register damage at a catalytic site readily, and about as readily at a matched site that is not catalytic; the predictor's apparent specificity for catalytic residues shrinks to about 1.2 once the distance to the ligand is controlled (3.4).

**Why "where" and not "what".** A catalytic site is a place before it is a chemistry. Catalytic residues sit together and next to the ligand, and a metric that reads local packing, burial or contact will register a change there whatever the reason for it. Three observations point to location. In the predictor's ladder (3.4), adjusting for the distance to the ligand removes 46 to 77% of the interface effect on the log scale, and the adjusted ratios are indistinguishable from 1. In the measured effects (3.3), metrics computed over the catalytic residues reach a median rank correlation of 0.44 with the measured impairment on BglB while metrics computed over the whole protein and sequence-only metrics sit at 0.09 and −0.02, and the distance from the mutated residue to the catalytic glutamates, which needs no structure prediction, reaches 0.46; within the near and distal strata of the binary comparison, where distance varies less, the AUROCs fall to 0.62 to 0.67. In the structure-space ladder (3.4), the largest ratios belong to relative solvent-accessible area and the fraction buried, which describe how a residue sits in the protein rather than what it does. <!-- src: results/P5_1B/REPORT.md summary item 2; results/D4_bglb/sensitivity_summary.csv; tables/supp/S04a_part1A_eligible_metrics.csv -->

We do not claim that location is all that the metrics see. The alternative reading is that they track the *magnitude of structural disturbance*, which grows with proximity and with lesion size; our data cannot tell the two apart. The structure-space experiment is the one that could, because its control is matched on burial, packing and residue type but not on distance to the site, and we did not run that check. A control matched on distance, as the predictor's ladder has, applied to the 143 enzymes would say whether the burial-descriptor and Rosetta ratios in Table S29 survive. Until then, the safe statement is the weaker one: the specific cells of the structure-space ladder show that a catalytic position differs from a matched non-catalytic position, not that the difference is catalytic.

**What the metrics are good for.** A critique that only subtracts leaves a practitioner with nothing, so we state what the evidence supports. The metrics detect gross damage: 19 of 26 structure-space metrics respond to truncating the catalytic residues to glycine, and 23 of 29 respond even to an isosteric substitution. They carry a position signal that is real, in that a substitution near the catalytic residues is more likely to impair kinetics in BglB and the catalytic-scoped metrics reflect it. These are sufficient for discarding structures that are plainly wrong and for flagging which substitutions are near the site. What the metrics lack is resolution, not validity: they do not distinguish a catalytic change from a nearby non-catalytic one, and that finer discrimination is what a shortlist of designs depends on. A response that is about location cannot rank two designs that differ in chemistry but not in position.

**Interface confidence and conditioned design.** Interface confidence is the family of quantities that responds most to which ligand was supplied: ipTM separates the cognate ligand from a wrong one with an AUROC of 0.65 to 0.75 in the 55 natural enzymes of the evaluation set and 0.87 to 0.91 in the de novo designs. But part of that is the size of the ligand (the cognate ligand scores higher in every cell in which the wrong ligand is much smaller, a win fraction of 1.00, and in 0.48 of cells in which it is larger), and the same-class wrong ligand is almost as detectable as the different-class one (0.69 against 0.75), and the whole-prediction pTM, which does not look at the ligand site, also changes (0.62). A growing class of generative methods conditions enzyme design on the substrate or the reaction [@song2024; @hua2024; @song2025; @song2026]. The evidence here does not show that the quantities such methods are scored on can separate a gain in reaction specificity from a gain in generic plausibility of the active site, and we know of no reported number that does. We do not claim that those methods fail, only that these metrics are not yet an instrument for showing that they succeed.

**Composition and the plates.** On the plated designs the best filter was a reference row, the tyrosine fraction, and 5 of 62 structure-space filters exceeded the chance band against about 3 expected from 62 draws at 5%. This agrees in direction with what was found for binder design, where residue fractions outperformed the structural confidence terms [@cotet2025]. We draw no inference from 16 active designs that lie in 8 backbone clusters, and we report the plates descriptively, as agreed before the analysis. They are consistent with the other three results and do not extend them.

**What we recommend.** None of the following needs new science.
- *Report the control, not only the response.* A claim that a metric is catalytic should carry a matched control on residue type and burial and on distance to the site. Our unadjusted interface ratios of about 2 were measured against a control that sat about 10 Å farther from the ligand than the residue it was matched to, and distance explains much of them.
- *Always show the trivial baselines.* Crystallographic resolution, sequence length, tyrosine fraction, predicted confidence and the distance to the catalytic residues are cheap to compute, and a metric that does not beat them has not been shown to read catalysis. In the dead-against-active comparison the two prediction-confidence reference rows (pLDDT 0.79, pTM 0.75) exceed every main-set metric (at most 0.61), and in the measured effects a distance baseline matched all of them.
- *Test a metric only where it is defined and can see the change.* 63 of the 191 distinct metrics in our panel are bookkeeping counters or constants, and sequence-only metrics have a specificity ratio of 1 by arithmetic under a matched control. Our applicability audit (Supplement, Tables S1–S6) separates what can be tested from what cannot, and the earlier whole-panel statistic of 0.995 included about 34 composition metrics whose ratio is 1 by construction.
- *Treat ligand size as a covariate* whenever interface confidence is used to compare substrates.

**Limitations.**
- *Computational lesions.* The perturbations are rotamer-space substitutions followed by a fixed-backbone repack and minimisation, with one energy function. 26 of 103 metrics move under the treatment alone; each arm is referenced to its own treated baseline, but a real mutant relaxes in ways this does not capture.
- *One predictor.* Every prediction-based result is Chai-1 with one seed per ladder prediction and no bitwise reproducibility. The seed replicate shows that the denominator of the specificity ratio is at the noise floor for the interface terms, so a single-seed ratio is uncertain by a quarter to a third.
- *The matched subset is a subpopulation.* 213 of 312 ladder pairs have no candidate control within the caliper anywhere in their protein, so the distance-matched result describes pairs for which a close control exists.
- *Small N.* The dead-against-active set has 21 pairs, all peptidases (the best-of-39 threshold has a median AUROC of 0.63), the measured effects have one enzyme, 175 positions and 16 active designs in 8 clusters, and cluster units for designs are proxies.
- *Labels.* The 176 no active plated designs are not measured negatives. A zymogen construct carries a propeptide (10.5 residues longer on average in the 21 pairs), so sequence length and predicted confidence are confounded with the label, although length alone does not explain the confidence difference (r = 0.14). The BglB reference structure for active-site accuracy carries a covalent intermediate rather than the substrate.
- *Ligands.* Only 2 of the 143 enzymes carry a ligand in the structure-space experiments, which removes the oxyanion-hole experiment and limits every interaction-dependent metric; the substrate experiments supply ligands by construction but run on 55 and 30 systems. A heme-type ligand that could not be built into a chemical structure was dropped by the predictor for four enzymes, which are excluded from the evaluation set.
- *Clumped actives.* 10 of the 16 active plated designs lie in two backbone clusters, and the best-of-36 threshold treats designs as exchangeable, so it understates the chance range (3.1.2).
- *Chosen wrong ligands.* The wrong ligands of the substrate swap are chosen by us, so its AUROCs describe sensitivity to the ligand supplied, and ligand size explains part of the interface signal (3.2).
- *Second-shell lesion and the pilot set.* No cell of the second-shell lesion is interpretable, and the 52 pilot enzymes pooled for the catalytic lesion have controls matched on residue type and burial only; adding them changes the mean R of a metric by at most 0.06 (median 0.02) (Tables 10, 11 and S28).
- *Selection of the zymogen pairs.* The 21 pairs share a ligand by construction, so they are more likely than a typical deposit to have a ligand-bound mature structure. The same-state null and the confound floor of the earlier analysis were computed on the 49 pairs and are not restated on 21; the reference rows on the same pairs take their place (the AUROC of every metric against the same-state null on the 49 pairs is in Table S7).
- *Folded variants only.* The destabilised, non-expressed and insufficient-data BglB variants are set aside, so the ranking describes the 432 folded variants and not all 655.
- *Correlated metrics.* The effective number of independent metrics is smaller than the count: the participation ratio is 7.9 of 29 for the native values and 10.5 of 29 for the isosteric responses (Table S46).
- *Dependence on the control.* The structure-space control is matched on burial, packing and local environment and not on distance to the catalytic site (its quality is in Tables S26 and S27), and the count of specific metrics depends on the control design: it differs without packing matching and arm-count gating and for the 30 de novo designs with a constructed control (Table S31; the burial of the constructed control is compared with that of the catalytic residues in Table S47).
- *Experiments that could not be made.* A deformation experiment (apparent effects tracked clash burden before repacking), the oxyanion-hole and metal-removal experiments, and a PLACER ensemble experiment (control coverage 1.6 to 3.2 times lower than the catalytic arm) are in the Supplement and are not reported as null results. <!-- src: tables/supp/S08_not_measurable.csv; results/P5_1B/PREREG_1B.md (amendments A1 to A7); results/D4_bglb/m3/REPORT.md section 6 -->

**What we do not claim.** Perturbation response is not activity prediction: a metric that responds to a lesion has not been shown to rank catalysts. A synthetic-negative AUROC, such as the wrong-ligand AUROC of 3.2, describes sensitivity to the ligand supplied and is not a measure of how well a metric ranks designs. A response measured on natural enzymes does not transfer to designs; whether it does is a separate hypothesis that we do not test. Active-site accuracy against a deposited structure and against a design's own model are different metrics, and we do not pool them.

**Outlook.** Three experiments would sharpen the result. A distance-matched control for the 143 enzymes would say whether the specific cells of the structure-space ladder are location. More enzymes with measured kinetics, analysed with the baselines declared here, would say whether the BglB pattern is general. And designs with measured activity, scored by the same panel before the results are known, would test the part of the claim that we explicitly leave open, the transfer from natural enzymes to designs. Until then, the practical reading is modest and actionable: the filters can tell that a site has been damaged and roughly where, they cannot yet tell that the damage was catalytic, and a design campaign that selects on them should declare the baselines they have to beat.
