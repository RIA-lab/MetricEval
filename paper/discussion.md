# 4 Discussion

**Principal finding.** The metrics that filter designed enzymes respond to where a change is made in a protein, and we could not show that they respond to whether the change is catalytic. Across four experiments with one panel of 34 metrics, the pattern is the same. The metrics register damage at a catalytic site readily, and they register it about as readily at a matched site that is not catalytic (3.1). Their apparent specificity for catalytic residues in a structure predictor shrinks to about 1.2 once the distance to the ligand is controlled (3.2). They do not separate real dead enzymes from active ones better than crystallographic resolution (3.3). And on 432 measured variants of one enzyme they carry a signal of AUROC about 0.75 that the distance from the mutated residue to the catalytic residues reproduces without any structure at all (3.4).

**Why "where" and not "what".** A catalytic site is a place before it is a chemistry. Catalytic residues sit together and next to the ligand, and a metric that reads local packing, burial or contact will register a change there whatever the reason for it. Three observations point to location. In 1B, adjusting for the distance to the ligand removes 46 to 77% of the interface effect on the log scale, and the adjusted ratios are indistinguishable from 1. In 2B, catalytic-scoped metrics reach a median AUROC of 0.75 on BglB while whole-protein and sequence-only metrics sit at 0.56 and 0.50, and the distance from the mutated residue to the catalytic glutamates, which needs no structure prediction, reaches 0.76; within the near and distal strata, where distance varies less, all values fall to 0.62 to 0.67. In 1A, the largest ratios belong to relative solvent-accessible area and the fraction buried, which describe how a residue sits in the protein rather than what it does. <!-- src: results/P5_1B/REPORT.md summary item 2; results/D4_bglb/sensitivity_summary.csv; tables/supp/S04a_part1A_eligible_metrics.csv -->

We do not claim that location is all that the metrics see. The alternative reading is that they track the *magnitude of structural disturbance*, which grows with proximity and with lesion size; our data cannot tell the two apart. The 1A experiment is the one that could, because its control is matched on burial, packing and residue type but not on distance to the site, and we did not run that check. A control matched on distance, as 1B has, applied to the 143 enzymes would say whether the burial-descriptor and Rosetta ratios in Table 3 survive. Until then, the safe statement is the weaker one: the specific cells of 1A show that a catalytic position differs from a matched non-catalytic position, not that the difference is catalytic.

**What the metrics are good for.** A critique that only subtracts leaves a practitioner with nothing, so we state what the evidence supports. The metrics detect gross damage: 19 of 26 structure-space metrics respond to truncating the catalytic residues to glycine, and 23 of 29 respond even to an isosteric substitution. They carry a position signal that is real, in that a substitution near the catalytic residues is more likely to impair kinetics in BglB and the catalytic-scoped metrics reflect it. These are sufficient for discarding structures that are plainly wrong and for flagging which substitutions are near the site. What the metrics lack is resolution, not validity: they do not distinguish a catalytic change from a nearby non-catalytic one, and that finer discrimination is what a shortlist of designs depends on. A response that is about location cannot rank two designs that differ in chemistry but not in position.

**Interface confidence and conditioned design.** Interface confidence is the one quantity in the panel that responds to which ligand was supplied: ipTM separates the cognate ligand from a wrong one with an AUROC of 0.67 to 0.74 in natural enzymes and 0.87 to 0.91 in designs. But part of that is the size of the ligand (the cognate ligand scores higher in every cell in which the wrong ligand is much smaller, a win fraction of 1.00, and in 0.48 of cells in which it is larger), and the same-class wrong ligand is almost as detectable as the different-class one (0.70 against 0.74). A growing class of generative methods conditions enzyme design on the substrate or the reaction [@song2024; @hua2024; @song2025; @song2026]. The evidence here does not show that the quantities such methods are scored on can separate a gain in reaction specificity from a gain in generic plausibility of the active site, and we know of no reported number that does. We do not claim that those methods fail, only that these metrics are not yet an instrument for showing that they succeed.

**Composition and the plates.** On the plated designs the best filter was a reference row, the tyrosine fraction, and 5 of 62 structure-space filters exceeded the chance band against about 3 expected from 62 draws at 5%. This agrees in direction with what was found for binder design, where residue fractions outperformed the structural confidence terms [@cotet2025]. We draw no inference from 16 active designs that lie in 8 backbone clusters, and we report the plates descriptively, as agreed before the analysis. They are consistent with the other three results and do not extend them.

**What we recommend.** None of the following needs new science.
- *Report the control, not only the response.* A claim that a metric is catalytic should carry a matched control on residue type and burial and on distance to the site. Our unadjusted interface ratios of about 2 were measured against a control that sat about 10 Å farther from the ligand than the residue it was matched to, and distance explains much of them.
- *Always show the trivial baselines.* Crystallographic resolution, sequence length, tyrosine fraction and the distance to the catalytic residues are cheap to compute, and a metric that does not beat them has not been shown to read catalysis. In 2A the point estimate of the best reference row (resolution, 0.61) is higher than that of every structure-space metric (at most 0.57), although the intervals overlap, and in 2B a distance baseline matched all of them.
- *Test a metric only where it is defined and can see the change.* 63 of the 191 distinct metrics in our panel are bookkeeping counters or constants, and sequence-only metrics have a specificity ratio of 1 by arithmetic under a matched control. Our applicability audit (Supplement, A1 to A5) separates what can be tested from what cannot, and the earlier whole-panel statistic of 0.995 included about 34 composition metrics whose ratio is 1 by construction.
- *Treat ligand size as a covariate* whenever interface confidence is used to compare substrates.

**Limitations.**
- *Computational lesions.* The perturbations are rotamer-space substitutions followed by a fixed-backbone repack and minimisation, with one energy function. 26 of 103 metrics move under the treatment alone; each arm is referenced to its own treated baseline, but a real mutant relaxes in ways this does not capture.
- *One predictor.* Every prediction-based result is Chai-1 with one seed per ladder prediction and no bitwise reproducibility. The seed replicate shows that the denominator of the specificity ratio is at the noise floor for the interface terms, so a single-seed ratio is uncertain by a quarter to a third.
- *The matched subset is a subpopulation.* 213 of 312 ladder pairs have no candidate control within the caliper anywhere in their protein, so the distance-matched result describes pairs for which a close control exists.
- *Small N.* 2A has 49 pairs (null standard error of an AUROC about 0.06), 2B has one enzyme, 175 positions and 16 active designs in 8 clusters, and cluster units for designs are proxies.
- *Labels.* The 176 plated designs without a reported activity are not measured negatives. A zymogen construct carries a propeptide, so sequence length and predicted confidence are confounded with the label. The BglB reference structure for active-site accuracy carries a covalent intermediate rather than the substrate.
- *Ligands.* Only 2 of the 143 enzymes carry a ligand in the structure-space experiments, which removes the oxyanion-hole experiment and limits every interaction-dependent metric; the substrate experiments supply ligands by construction but run on 59 and 30 systems. A heme that could not be parsed was dropped by the predictor in one system, which we excluded.
- *Experiments that could not be made.* A deformation axis (apparent effects tracked clash burden before repacking), the oxyanion-hole and metal-removal experiments, and a PLACER ensemble experiment (control coverage 1.6 to 3.2 times lower than the catalytic arm) are in the Supplement and are not reported as null results. <!-- src: tables/supp/S08_not_measurable.csv; results/P5_1B/PREREG_1B.md (amendments A1 to A7); results/D4_bglb/m3/REPORT.md section 6 -->
- *Corrections made while building the tables.* A legacy ordering file averaged each metric over all five null conditions, including an unrelated protein, and was replaced by a corrected analysis; the zymogen re-prediction was scored before the definedness gate and its interface values are flagged; and one earlier whole-panel statistic included composition metrics. These are in the Supplement with the artifacts that show them. <!-- src: tables/spec/derived/p6_activity_ranking_native.csv; tables/supp/S18_T2_restated.csv; tables/supp/S11_equivalence_legacy_vs_main.csv -->

**What we do not claim.** Perturbation response is not activity prediction: a metric that responds to a lesion has not been shown to rank catalysts. A synthetic-negative AUROC, such as the wrong-ligand AUROC of 3.2, describes sensitivity to the ligand supplied and is not a measure of how well a metric ranks designs. A response measured on natural enzymes does not transfer to designs; whether it does is a separate hypothesis that we do not test. Active-site accuracy against a deposited structure and against a design's own model are different metrics, and we do not pool them.

**Outlook.** Three experiments would sharpen the result. A distance-matched control for the 143 enzymes would say whether the specific cells of 1A are location. More enzymes with measured kinetics, analysed with the baselines declared here, would say whether the BglB pattern is general. And designs with measured activity, scored by the same panel before the results are known, would test the part of the claim that we explicitly leave open, the transfer from natural enzymes to designs. Until then, the practical reading is modest and actionable: the filters can tell that a site has been damaged and roughly where, they cannot yet tell that the damage was catalytic, and a design campaign that selects on them should declare the baselines they have to beat.
